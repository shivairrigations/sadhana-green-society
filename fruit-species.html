Fruit species for agroforestry

Fruit trees for steady income and better nutrition, with their benefits, best-fit land and uses.

Fruit trees give farm families regular income and healthy food, along with shade and carbon storage. These are the fruit species we most often recommend.

  • Mango
  • Guava
  • Sapota
  • Custard apple
  • Amla
  • Lemon
  • Coconut
  • Cashew

Mango

మామిడిMangifera indica

Long-lived orchard tree with seasonal income.

Best for: Deep, well-drained soils. Orchards with intercrops in the early years.

Benefits

  • Regular seasonal income for many years
  • Large canopy stores carbon and gives shade
  • Local varieties such as Banganapalli have strong market demand
  • Flowers support bees and other pollinators

Main uses

  • Fresh fruit
  • Pickles
  • Pulp

Guava

జామPsidium guajava

Hardy fruit tree that bears early.

Best for: A wide range of soils, including lighter soils. Good for small farms.

Benefits

  • Starts fruiting early, so returns come sooner
  • Hardy, with modest input needs
  • Rich in vitamin C and good for household nutrition
  • Sold fresh or processed

Main uses

  • Fresh fruit
  • Jam
  • Juice

Sapota (Chikoo)

సపోటాManilkara zapota

Evergreen tree with fruit through much of the year.

Best for: Well-drained soils with irrigation. Also suited to coastal areas.

Benefits

  • Fruits over a long season, so income is steadier
  • Evergreen canopy gives year-round shade
  • Long-lived tree that stores carbon
  • Adapts to many soil types

Main uses

  • Fresh fruit
  • Milkshake

Custard apple

సీతాఫలంAnnona squamosa

Drought-hardy fruit for dry, rainfed land.

Best for: Dry, rocky and rainfed land such as upland Rayalaseema.

Benefits

  • Needs little water and low input
  • Grows on poor and sloping land
  • Fruit has strong seasonal demand
  • Earns income from land unsuitable for many crops

Main uses

  • Fresh fruit
  • Pulp

Amla (Indian gooseberry)

ఉసిరిPhyllanthus emblica

Very hardy fruit tree with high-value processed products.

Best for: Dry and rainfed land with light to medium soils.

Benefits

  • Tolerates drought and poor soils
  • One of the richest natural sources of vitamin C
  • Processed products fetch better prices than fresh fruit
  • Low maintenance once established

Main uses

  • Pickle
  • Juice
  • Candy
  • Powder

Lemon

నిమ్మCitrus limon

Small tree with quick, regular returns.

Best for: Well-drained soils with reliable irrigation.

Benefits

  • Starts bearing early and fruits through much of the year
  • Steady local market demand
  • Compact size suits small farms and field boundaries
  • Vitamin C for household nutrition

Main uses

  • Fresh fruit
  • Pickle
  • Juice

Coconut

కొబ్బరిCocos nucifera

Multi-purpose palm for coastal and irrigated land.

Best for: Coastal and irrigated land. Other crops can be grown between palms.

Benefits

  • Nuts, oil, fibre and leaves all have uses
  • Long-lived palm that stores carbon
  • Space between palms allows intercropping
  • Harvest comes round the year

Main uses

  • Nuts
  • Oil
  • Fibre

Cashew

జీడిమామిడిAnacardium occidentale

Low-input tree for poor and sloping land.

Best for: Gravelly, sloping and coastal soils where other crops are difficult.

Benefits

  • Grows on poor soil with low input
  • Nuts have strong market demand
  • Helps prevent erosion on slopes
  • The cashew apple can be processed

Main uses

  • Nuts
  • Cashew apple

Choosing the right trees

Species suit different soils, rainfall and markets. Our field team helps each farmer choose a mix that fits their land.

Mixing a few species spreads risk: fruit trees give yearly income, timber trees build long-term value, and nitrogen-fixing trees improve the soil.
