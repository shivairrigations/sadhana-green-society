Forest species for agroforestry

Timber, pulpwood and soil-building trees we promote on farmland, with their benefits and uses.

Forest trees give farmers long-term value from timber and poles while protecting soil, storing carbon and giving shade. Here are the species we most often discuss with farmers.

  • Teak
  • Melia dubia
  • Casuarina
  • Pongamia
  • Neem
  • Bamboo
  • Subabul
  • Red sanders

Teak

టేకుTectona grandis

Premium timber tree for long-term income.

Best for: Deep, well-drained soils with reliable moisture. Block plantations and farm boundaries.

Benefits

  • Very high-value timber for furniture and construction
  • Stores large amounts of carbon in long-lived wood
  • Fallen leaves add organic matter to the soil
  • Long-term income that works like a family savings plan

Main uses

  • Timber
  • Furniture
  • Poles

Note: Slow growing. Income comes at harvest after many years, so grow intercrops in the early years.

Melia dubia

Also called Malabar neem

Fast-growing timber for plywood and pulp.

Best for: Farm bunds and block plantations, with irrigation or good rainfall.

Benefits

  • Grows quickly, so income comes sooner than from slow timber trees
  • Timber is in demand for plywood, packing and pulp
  • Captures carbon fast while it grows
  • Light canopy in the early years allows intercropping

Main uses

  • Plywood
  • Packing
  • Pulpwood

Note: Needs water and protection from grazing animals while young.

Casuarina

సరుగుడుCasuarina equisetifolia

Hardy tree for sandy and coastal land.

Best for: Sandy, coastal and salt-affected soils. Windbreaks and block plantations.

Benefits

  • Tolerates sandy and salty conditions where many crops struggle
  • Fixes nitrogen and improves poor soil
  • Works as a windbreak that protects crops
  • Straight poles and pulpwood sell well

Main uses

  • Poles
  • Pulpwood
  • Fuelwood

Pongamia

కానుగMillettia pinnata

Drought-hardy shade tree that improves soil.

Best for: Dry and rainfed land, field boundaries, roadsides and degraded land.

Benefits

  • Withstands drought and poor soils
  • Fixes nitrogen, which helps crops growing nearby
  • Seeds give oil, and the seed cake can be used as organic manure
  • Dense shade and a cooler farm microclimate

Main uses

  • Seed oil
  • Organic manure
  • Shade

Neem

వేపAzadirachta indica

Tough, useful tree with many farm and household uses.

Best for: Almost everywhere in the region, including dry and rainfed land.

Benefits

  • Survives dry spells and low-fertility soil
  • Leaves and seed extracts are used as natural pest control
  • Good shade and useful timber
  • Traditional medicinal and household uses

Main uses

  • Natural pesticide
  • Shade
  • Timber

Bamboo

వెదురుBambusa and Dendrocalamus species

Fast-growing grass harvested without felling the clump.

Best for: Farm boundaries, riverbanks and block plantations with enough moisture.

Benefits

  • Very fast growth and strong carbon capture
  • Culms can be harvested repeatedly without replanting
  • Roots bind soil and reduce erosion
  • Used in construction, crafts and pulp

Main uses

  • Construction
  • Crafts
  • Pulp

Subabul

సుబాబుల్Leucaena leucocephala

Quick pulpwood and fuelwood tree that fixes nitrogen.

Best for: Rainfed and irrigated farmland, commonly grown as pulpwood.

Benefits

  • Fast growth gives early returns
  • Pulpwood is bought by paper mills
  • Fixes nitrogen, which benefits the soil
  • Branches give fuelwood

Main uses

  • Pulpwood
  • Fuelwood

Red sanders

ఎర్రచందనంPterocarpus santalinus

Native Eastern Ghats tree of very high value.

Best for: Dry, rocky, well-drained soils in the Eastern Ghats region.

Benefits

  • Very valuable heartwood
  • Native species that supports local biodiversity
  • Tolerates dry and poor soils
  • Strong long-term value for farmers who plant it legally

Main uses

  • Heartwood
  • Native species

Note: Cultivation, harvest and transport are regulated. Check with the Forest Department before planting.

Choosing the right trees

Species suit different soils, rainfall and markets. Our field team helps each farmer choose a mix that fits their land.

Mixing a few species spreads risk: fruit trees give yearly income, timber trees build long-term value, and nitrogen-fixing trees improve the soil.
